Gaeden Evans

Finance, Howard University. Class of 2029.

Applying to Recalc.

My family's money problem was never income. It was knowledge — nobody taught us how money works. I got interested in how wealthy people actually use capital to make more of it, and decided I'd rather learn it properly than guess. That's the whole reason I'm in finance.

  • The DCF

    I could build one before I understood a single line of it. NOI, cap rates, rent rolls all made sense immediately. The DCF I was just operating.

    What changed

    A company is worth the cash it will hand you, minus the fact that cash later is worth less than cash now. Every row in the model is that one sentence, itemized. I got there by finding upperclassmen who already knew it and making them explain it until the reason showed up, not the steps.

  • Being the advanced one

    At Greenwood I've been ahead of my peers on technicals. I don't like it.

    Why not

    Mostly it means I asked for help earlier than they did. Being called advanced sets a ceiling I haven't earned, and it makes people stop telling me where my holes are — which is the only information I actually want. Still patching.

  • Casual and professional at the same time

    Networking taught me the tactical parts. When to follow up, how to find someone's contact information. This is the one I haven't solved.

Last year I gave someone my notes for the Howard Investment Group application while I was applying to it myself. He got in. I didn't. I'm applying again this year, and I helped someone else again.

From the bottom, the top of a mountain looks like a point. Climb one and the top is flat. There's more room up there than it looks like from down here.
